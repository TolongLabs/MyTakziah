## Read This First

This guide exists because issue #5 is the only part of the submission that cannot be researched, and the rubric specifically penalises AI-generated statements presented as user research. **Nothing in this file may be answered by an AI, and nothing may be filled in from imagination.** If the conversation does not happen, see "If No Conversation Happens" at the bottom, which tells you exactly how Section 2 survives without it.

Write the results into `user-insight-bereavement.md`, which is already scaffolded.

## Who To Approach

Any one of these is enough. One conversation is the target, not five.

| Who                                 | What They Give You                                        | Watch For                                                          |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A family member who handled a death | First-hand depth on documents, trips, and what was missed | Emotional load. Be ready to stop.                                  |
| A friend's parent                   | Same, with more distance                                  | Ask the friend to introduce you first                              |
| A khairat kematian committee member | Breadth across many families                              | Their account of a family's feelings is **second-hand**. Label it. |
| A neighbour or colleague            | Convenience, speed                                        | Do not push if they decline                                        |

A khairat committee member is the fastest route if no family member is available, and they usually know exactly which documents families struggle with. Just be honest in the write-up about which parts are first-hand and which are reported.

## Consent Script

Say this before anything else. Written notes only. **Do not record audio unless they explicitly say yes.**

> Before we start: I am a student working on a competition entry about the government paperwork families have to deal with after a death. I would like to write down what you tell me. I will not use your name, your family's name, or anything that identifies you. If I want to quote a sentence of yours word for word, I will ask you first. You can stop at any time, or tell me not to write something down. Is that alright?

## The Method, In One Line

**Ask about what they actually did. Never about what they would want.**

## The Conversation, 25 Minutes

### Opening, 3 Minutes

- "Can you tell me about when your [relative] passed away? I do not need details about them. I am asking about what you had to deal with afterwards."
- "Who in the family ended up handling the paperwork?"

### Walk The Timeline, 10 Minutes

This is the core. Keep them moving forward in time and stay quiet.

- "Start from the day it happened. What was the first office or counter you had to deal with?"
- "Then what?" Repeat this. It is the most productive question in the guide.
- "How long did the whole thing take, from the death to the last thing you had to sort out?"

### Documents And Trips, 5 Minutes

The first two are issue #5's questions 1 and 2, unchanged.

- "What did you have to produce, and how many times?"
- "Where did you physically have to go?"
- "Did you ever have to go back because something was missing?"
- "Was any of it possible online, or did it all have to be in person?"

### Misses And Gaps, 5 Minutes

Issue #5's questions 3 and 4.

- "What did you nearly miss, or miss?"
- "What did nobody tell you?"
- "How did you find out what you were entitled to?"

### The Hardest Part, 2 Minutes

Issue #5's question 5.

- "What was hardest in the first week?"
- "If you had to do it again for someone else, what would you do differently?"

### Close

- "Is there anything I should have asked but did not?"
- Point at any sentence you want to quote and ask: "Can I use that sentence exactly as you said it?"
- **Only now** explain what you are building, and ask what they think. Anything they say after this point is a reaction to your pitch, not evidence. Label it that way.

## Probes To Hold In Reserve

Use these **only if the participant raises the topic themselves**. Asking them cold turns the interview into a survey that confirms what we already believe, which is worse than no interview.

| If They Mention      | You May Ask                                                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| EPF or KWSP          | "Did anyone tell you there was a time limit?"                      |
| More than one agency | "Did any of them already have the information from the other one?" |
| Documents generally  | "Which document did you end up showing the most times?"            |
| PERKESO or SOCSO     | "Did you know beforehand whether you would qualify?"               |
| Going in person      | "Did you have to bring originals, or were copies enough?"          |

## Do Not Ask These

Each of these produces evidence a judge is entitled to throw out.

- "Would you use an app that did all of this for you?" Hypothetical. Worthless. The rubric asks whether proposed metrics are future measures rather than fabricated results, and the same scepticism applies to user claims.
- "Wasn't it frustrating to give the same documents again and again?" Leading. You have handed them the answer.
- "Don't you think the government should link these systems?" That is pitching, not interviewing.
- Anything that invites them to agree with the shared-fields matrix. The matrix is already evidenced from the forms themselves and does not need their confirmation. What we need from them is what the forms cannot tell us.

## Write It Up Within 30 Minutes

Memory degrades fast and reconstructed detail is the main way an honest note becomes a dishonest one.

Open `user-insight-bereavement.md` and fill it in. As you write, tag every single item:

- **Verbatim** for exact words, only where you have consent.
- **Close note** for accurate paraphrase written during or right after the conversation.
- **Reconstructed** for anything recalled later.

If you cannot remember whether something was verbatim, it is a close note. If you cannot remember whether they said it at all, it does not go in.

## What One Conversation Can And Cannot Support

Get this right and Section 2 is stronger than a competitor's fabricated focus group. Get it wrong and the whole submission looks unreliable.

**It can support:**

- An existence claim: "In one recorded conversation, a Malaysian who handled a death described [X]."
- Concrete illustrative detail that desk research cannot produce, such as how many trips, in what order, what surprised them.
- **Disconfirmation.** If they say the process was fine, that is a real finding and it goes in. Do not discard it.

**It cannot support:**

- How common any of it is.
- What "most survivors" or "families in Malaysia" experience.
- Any percentage, average, or count beyond this one account.
- What users want, or that anyone would use MyTakziah.

## If No Conversation Happens

The deadline does not move, so decide by **Thursday morning** and do not let this block Section 2.

Section 2 survives without an interview, on one condition: **it must not imply user research it does not have.**

Fallback wording, safe to use as-is:

> We have not conducted primary user research within the competition window. Section 2 is built from the published requirements of the three agencies, recorded in our shared-fields matrix, and from national statistics.

Then build the section from what is already verified:

- The survivor is typically an older spouse, with median age at death at 69.0 years (`verified-figures.md`).
- Among Malaysians who use the internet, 33.6 per cent of those aged 60 and over interact with government organisations online, against 38.9 per cent overall, while 91.6 per cent of that age group are internet users at all. Connected, but least likely to transact.
- The survivor must attend in person, carrying original documents, at more than one agency (`shared-fields-matrix.md`).
- One verified deadline sits on them, the EPF six-month window (`agency-rule-map.md`).

A judge who reads an honest disclosure of a missing method trusts the rest of the document. A judge who catches an invented quotation stops reading. **The fallback is not a failure state. Inventing the interview is.**
