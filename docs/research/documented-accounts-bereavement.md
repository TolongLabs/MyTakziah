## The Pattern Worth Reporting

Malaysian outlets have independently reported survivors collecting death benefits **two decades
after the death**, in cases where the entitlement existed the whole time.

The failure mode in these accounts is **not rejection**. It is that nobody told the family, or
the family had no way to check.

That is precisely the gap MyTakziah addresses, and it is the one claim this file supports well.

## Account 1: Twenty-Three Years, Because Nobody Told Her

**Tan Gek Hiang**, 58, a cleaner living in Tabuan Laru, Kuching, collected her SOCSO survivor
benefits **almost 23 years after her husband died** of an illness. She also began receiving a
lifetime monthly survivor's pension.

Quoted from the report: she **"had not been denied from claiming the benefits, but two decades had
passed before she got to know through her sister that she was eligible for it."** Her sister
learned of the entitlement at a community seminar and persuaded her to apply.

## Account 2: Twenty-One Years, And It Took An Insider To Find Out

**Zaliha Yunus** received **RM106,732.79 from SOCSO in 2023, 21 years after her husband died in 2002.** She raised her children on RM100 a month in the interim: _"I had struggled with a monthly
income of RM100 a month to bring up my children."_

**Read this case carefully. It is not the same failure as Account 1.** The stated reason for the
delay was that _"his employer had not made Socso contributions since 1997 although he was
registered as a member"_. That is an employer compliance failure, not a knowledge failure.

**What makes it relevant anyway** is how it was eventually resolved: _"her relative, who is a Socso
employee, checked the status of her husband's record again and found that the employer had settled
all outstanding payments in 2017"_.

The employer settled in **2017**. She was paid in **2023**. **Six years passed in which she was
entitled and did not know, and what closed the gap was having a relative who worked inside the
agency.**

## Account 3: An Official Naming Awareness As The Problem

Mohammad Ramli, Selangor SOCSO deputy director, quoted by Bernama:

> "We only hope that Socso contributors and the beneficiaries are aware of their rights and would
> contact us immediately in the event of death or should they be stricken with chronic illness."

## Official Confirmation Of How Many Bodies Are Involved

The Malaysian government's own portal sets out what a family must do after a death, and names,
across its guidance: **JPN, the Ministry of Health, PDRM, DBKL, Malaysian embassies for overseas
deaths, the High Court for presumption of death, and State Zakat Boards.**

It also carries **multiple separate deadlines**, including:

| Deadline                  | Applies To                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **6 months after death**  | EPF death benefit                                                 |
| **12 months after death** | Funeral assistance                                                |
| 40 days, then 6 months    | Deaths during Hajj, to the Hedjaz registrar then JPN headquarters |
| More than 7 years         | Presumption of death for a missing person                         |

| Field         | Entry                                                                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Publisher     | Government of Malaysia, MyGovernment portal                                     |
| Title         | Dealing with Death                                                              |
| Last Updated  | 27 July 2026                                                                    |
| Location      | https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/categories/death--inheritance/dealing-with-death |
| Accessed      | 2026-08-19                                                                      |
| Evidence Type | Primary, official government portal                                             |

**This is the strongest single citation in this file**, because it is official, current, and shows
the fragmentation in the government's own words rather than ours.

**New finding for the rule map:** the **12-month funeral assistance deadline** was not previously
recorded. It should be added to the deadline board in `agency-rule-map.md` after confirmation of
which scheme it attaches to.

## Supporting, Weak

An academic review of khairat kematian management in Malaysia concludes that **"many lack
knowledge about preparing to deal with death"** and calls for awareness and education.

Shahbuddin, Z. H. S., Gunardi, S., Ab. Rahman, A., Salleh, A. Z., Syed Omar, S. M. N., and Mohd
Noor, K., "Evolusi Pengurusan Khairat Kematian Di Malaysia: Satu Tinjauan", _Al-Qanatir:
International Journal of Islamic Studies_, Vol. 33 No. 5, 16 September 2024,
https://al-qanatir.com/aq/article/view/961, accessed 2026-08-19. Open access, CC BY 4.0.

**Use sparingly.** The paper is about digitalising community funeral funds, not about government
administrative burden. The single quoted phrase is the only part that supports our claim.

## The Trap In This Evidence Base

Search repeatedly surfaces **RM65 billion to RM90 billion in frozen or unclaimed Malaysian
inheritance assets**, attributed to Amanah Raya and reported widely.

**Do not use it.** Estate distribution, faraid and probate are **explicitly out of scope** by the
recorded decision of 2026-08-19. Citing it would contradict our own scope statement in the same
document, and the first judge who noticed would be right to.

It is a genuinely tempting number. That is exactly why it is written down here as a trap.

## Honest Limits, To Be Stated Rather Than Hidden

- **These are three reported cases plus one official portal.** Journalism selects for the
  remarkable, so 23-year delays are reported precisely because they are unusual. **This evidence
  cannot establish how common the problem is, and we do not claim it does.**
- **Two of the three accounts are from 2017 and 2020.** They are dated.
- **The 23-year case has not been opened at source by anyone.** PressReader is paywalled and
  the Borneo Post original blocks automated retrieval. **Until someone opens it in a browser,
  Section 2 must not quote it.** The safe fallback is below.
- **Account 2 has a different root cause** to Account 1 and must not be blended with it.
- **No Malaysian measurement exists** of how long bereavement administration takes or how many
  claims are missed. That gap remains open and is stated in Section 5.
