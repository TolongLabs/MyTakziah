# DataKampung
## A Community-Owned AI Cooperative for Rural Malaysia

**Competition:** URIIS Student Biz Innov Challenge 2026  
**Challenge Area:** Empowering Rural Communities Through Innovation  
**Core Idea:** Rural communities should not only *use* AI. They should be able to **own, govern, and benefit economically from the local data and knowledge that make AI useful.**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**DataKampung** is a community-owned AI cooperative that helps rural communities turn their local knowledge and non-sensitive community data into shared digital assets.

Instead of allowing outside organisations to collect local data, build AI products, and capture most of the value, DataKampung gives the community a structured way to:

1. **Collect useful local data and knowledge**
2. **Decide collectively how it may be used**
3. **Approve or reject requests from universities, companies, NGOs, or government agencies**
4. **License approved datasets or AI services**
5. **Receive income or community benefits from approved uses**
6. **Co-own locally useful AI tools created from the community's contribution**

The long-term vision is simple:

> **The next AI system built using rural Malaysia's knowledge should create value for rural Malaysians too.**

DataKampung changes rural communities from passive users of technology into **owners and partners in the AI economy**.

---

# 2. The Problem

AI systems are becoming increasingly dependent on high-quality, local, context-rich data.

However, rural communities often hold valuable information that is:

- fragmented across individuals and organisations,
- recorded manually,
- not digitised,
- difficult for researchers or companies to access,
- collected without clear long-term benefit to the community,
- underrepresented in mainstream AI datasets.

Examples include:

- crop and harvest patterns,
- local pest observations,
- weather and flooding experiences,
- local market prices,
- supply chain information,
- farming practices,
- small-business knowledge,
- local terminology and language usage,
- community service needs,
- local transportation patterns.

The issue is not simply a lack of AI access.

The deeper issue is:

> **Rural communities may provide the knowledge that makes AI useful, while receiving little ownership or economic participation in the value created from it.**

This creates an imbalance.

### Current pattern

```text
Community knowledge
        ↓
External organisation collects data
        ↓
Organisation builds dataset / AI model
        ↓
Organisation owns most of the resulting value
        ↓
Community becomes an end user
```

### DataKampung model

```text
Community knowledge
        ↓
Community-owned cooperative
        ↓
Community decides access and conditions
        ↓
Approved data / AI collaboration
        ↓
Value is created
        ↓
Community shares in the benefits
```

# 4. The Core Innovation

The innovation is **not** another AI chatbot for rural communities.

The innovation is a new ownership model.

## Traditional Rural AI

> "Here is an AI tool that can help villagers."

## DataKampung

> "The community owns part of the data, knowledge, and AI value chain itself."

This creates three layers of ownership.

---

## Layer 1 — Community Data Ownership

Participating communities decide:

- what information can be contributed,
- what information cannot be collected,
- who can request access,
- what purposes are acceptable,
- how long access lasts,
- what benefits are expected in return.

---

## Layer 2 — Community AI Ownership

If a useful AI system is created using DataKampung assets, the community can negotiate participation in the resulting value.

Possible arrangements include:

- revenue sharing,
- licensing income,
- free community access,
- discounted services,
- co-ownership of selected digital assets,
- community development contributions.

---

## Layer 3 — Community Decision-Making

The platform should not allow one individual or company to decide everything.

Each participating cooperative can have a simple digital governance process.

Example:

```text
Company submits request
        ↓
DataKampung explains request in simple language
        ↓
Community committee reviews
        ↓
Members vote or approve through agreed governance rules
        ↓
Approved / Rejected / Changes requested
```

# 5. How DataKampung Works

## Step 1 — A Community Creates a DataKampung Cooperative

A farming cooperative, village group, or rural association joins DataKampung.

The group creates its profile.

Example:

**Kampung Seri Maju Agricultural Cooperative**

Members: 180 farmers  
Main crops: chilli, banana, vegetables  
Location: Rural Malaysia  
Community goal: Improve crop productivity and create new income opportunities

---

## Step 2 — The Community Identifies Valuable Knowledge

DataKampung helps the community identify information that could create value.

Example categories:

- crop disease observations,
- planting schedules,
- yield information,
- rainfall observations,
- local produce prices,
- logistics costs,
- farming methods.

The platform should prioritise **non-sensitive, de-identified, aggregated, or community-level information** wherever possible.

---

## Step 3 — Data Is Organised Into Community Data Assets

Instead of a messy collection of spreadsheets and notebooks, DataKampung creates structured "Community Data Assets".

Example:

### Chilli Farming Dataset

**Records:** 14,500 observations  
**Contributors:** 83 farmers  
**Period:** 2024–2026  
**Contains:**

- planting date,
- weather condition,
- pest observation,
- treatment used,
- harvest result.

**Does not contain:**

- identity card numbers,
- bank information,
- precise personal financial information.

---

# 6. The Data Passport

Every community dataset receives a simple **Data Passport**.

This makes the platform understandable even for non-technical users.

Example:

## Chilli Farming Data Passport

**Owned by:** Kampung Seri Maju Cooperative

**Contributors:** 83 farmers

**Purpose:**
Improve crop disease prediction and farming productivity.

**Approved uses:**

- agricultural research,
- crop disease prediction,
- community agricultural planning.

**Not approved:**

- advertising,
- personal credit scoring,
- resale to unrelated third parties.

**Benefit-sharing rule:**

40% contributors  
30% community fund  
20% platform and data maintenance  
10% education and digital training

The exact percentages would be decided by each cooperative.

# 7. The Data Request Marketplace

Researchers, companies, NGOs, and agencies can submit a request.

Example:

## Request from AgriTech X

> We want to train an AI model that predicts chilli disease using leaf observations, weather conditions, and historical crop outcomes.

### Request details

**Dataset requested:** Chilli Farming Dataset  
**Purpose:** Disease prediction AI  
**Duration:** 12 months  
**Commercial use:** Yes  
**Proposed payment:** RM30,000  
**Benefit offered:** Free AI tool for participating farmers for 2 years

DataKampung converts the request into simple language before presenting it to community representatives.

---

# 8. AI-Assisted Community Decision Support

Many data-licensing agreements are too complex for ordinary users.

DataKampung can use AI to translate a proposal into simple questions.

Instead of showing a 20-page agreement, the platform explains:

> **What does this company want?**  
> It wants to use three years of chilli farming observations to train a crop disease AI.

> **Can the company identify individual farmers?**  
> No personal identities are included in the proposed dataset.

> **Will the company make money from the AI?**  
> Yes.

> **What does the community receive?**  
> RM30,000 plus free access to the resulting tool for two years.

> **Can the company sell the original dataset?**  
> No.

The community can then:

**Approve**  
**Reject**  
**Request better terms**

---

# 9. Benefit-Sharing Engine

If a request is approved, DataKampung automatically records the agreed benefit-sharing structure.

Example:

## RM30,000 licensing income

| Recipient | Share | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Data contributors | 40% | RM12,000 |
| Community development fund | 30% | RM9,000 |
| Data maintenance | 10% | RM3,000 |
| Digital/AI education | 10% | RM3,000 |
| Platform operations | 10% | RM3,000 |

This is only an example.

Different communities could create different rules.

The important principle is:

> **People who contribute to AI value creation should be able to participate in the value created.**

# 10. From Data Cooperative to AI Cooperative

This is where DataKampung becomes more ambitious.

The long-term goal is not only to license datasets.

It is to enable communities to own or co-own **AI assets**.

For example:

A university and DataKampung create:

### CiliGuard AI

An AI model that predicts potential chilli crop disease.

Instead of the university or company owning everything, the agreement might state:

- university owns the research IP,
- technology company commercialises the system,
- participating cooperative receives a royalty on Malaysian commercial deployments,
- community members receive free access,
- part of revenue funds future community data collection.

Now the economic structure becomes:

```text
Community contributes knowledge
        ↓
AI model is created
        ↓
AI becomes commercially useful
        ↓
Customers pay
        ↓
Part of the value returns to the community
```

This transforms rural communities from **data sources** into **AI stakeholders**.

# 15. Who Owns What?

The ownership structure must be easy to understand.

## Individual

The individual retains rights over information they contribute according to the cooperative's rules and applicable agreements.

## Cooperative

The cooperative controls the collective community data asset and decides how approved shared assets may be used.

## DataKampung

DataKampung provides the infrastructure, governance tools, marketplace, and benefit-sharing system.

It should **not automatically become the owner of all community data**.

## AI Partner

A university or company may own specific technology it develops, but the commercial agreement can still include:

- community usage rights,
- revenue share,
- access conditions,
- limits on data reuse.

---

# 16. Trust and Safety Principles

The idea will be much stronger if trust is built into the concept from day one.

DataKampung should follow five principles.

## 1. Consent

People should understand what they are contributing and why.

## 2. Minimum Necessary Data

Collect only what is useful for the agreed purpose.

## 3. Community Control

External organisations cannot simply download data without approval.

## 4. Transparent Benefit Sharing

Participants can see where revenue goes.

## 5. Traceability

The community can see:

- who requested data,
- what was approved,
- why it was approved,
- how long access lasts,
- what benefits were promised.

### Proposal Simplification

Match community datasets with relevant research or commercial opportunities.

# 25. Risks and Mitigation

## Risk 1 — Low Data Quality

**Mitigation:**  
Data quality checks, structured forms, assisted data collection, contributor training.

---

## Risk 2 — Community Members Do Not Understand Agreements

**Mitigation:**  
AI-generated plain-language summaries plus human/community representatives.

---

## Risk 3 — Unfair Commercial Deals

**Mitigation:**  
Standardised agreement templates, transparent benefit comparison, collective approval.

---

## Risk 4 — Sensitive Data Is Accidentally Collected

**Mitigation:**  
Data minimisation, de-identification, clear prohibited categories, review before publication or sharing.

---

## Risk 5 — Low Participation

**Mitigation:**  
Start with existing cooperatives or organised community groups rather than creating communities from scratch.

---

## Risk 6 — Companies Do Not Want to Pay

**Mitigation:**  
Focus first on high-value, difficult-to-obtain local datasets with clear research or commercial usefulness.

# 26. Why Start With Existing Cooperatives

The most practical launch strategy is not:

> "Convince an entire village to join a new technology platform."

Instead:

> **Partner with an existing agricultural cooperative or organised rural association.**

They already have:

- members,
- leadership,
- community trust,
- decision-making structures,
- shared economic interests.

DataKampung becomes the **digital and AI layer** on top of an organisation that already exists.

This makes the concept much more feasible.

---

# 27. Stronger Positioning

Avoid positioning DataKampung as:

> "A marketplace for selling rural data."

That sounds extractive and creates unnecessary trust concerns.

Better:

> **A community-owned AI cooperative.**

Or:

> **Infrastructure that lets rural communities own and benefit from the AI economy.**

The emphasis should always remain:

**ownership + governance + benefit sharing + local AI creation**

rather than simply monetising information.
