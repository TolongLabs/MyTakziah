## Part 1: What Happens Today

Someone's father dies. Three agencies want the same five things, separately.

Who died. Who you are. Proof of the death. How you are related. Where the money goes.

| Step | Where   | What It Costs The Family                                           |
| ---- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1    | JPN     | In person, at the registration office, carrying original documents |
| 2    | PERKESO | In person again, a different office, the same originals            |
| 3    | KWSP    | A third form, the same five things, on paper                       |

Three agencies. Three different forms: **JPN.LM02**, **KWSP 9KM (AHL)**, and **Borang 34** or **Borang 24 and 26**.

**The government already knows he died, because the family told one part of it. It just did not tell the other two.**

### It Gets Worse If The Family Is Not A Standard Shape

PERKESO's relationship evidence is tiered. A family that is not spouse-and-children must produce documents about other people: parents' identity documents and siblings' birth certificates, and at the furthest tier, the parents' death certificates and the grandparents' identity documents.

**The further a family sits from the standard shape, the more documents about more people it must chase, at exactly the moment it is least able to.**

### The Part That Survives Even In The Worst Case

Our integration with the agencies is **proposed, not proven**. Assume no agency ever agrees and MyTakziah can only print pre-filled forms the family carries. **Three things still change, and they are the three that cost families money:**

1. **You know the benefits exist.**

3. **You arrive with the right documents, so you are not rejected and sent home.**

**The queuing is what everyone notices. It is not what costs anything.** A family can survive three queues. It cannot survive not knowing.

## Part 3: Three Proofs This Is Not A Technology Gap

**This is the strongest section and the one most likely to win the room.** Each agency has already built something sophisticated. None of them built this.

**EPF lets a member nominate a beneficiary from a phone**, identity verified by e-KYC, no office visit. **The beneficiary is offered nothing.** The only action on KWSP's Death Withdrawal page is a link labelled "Apply For Manual Withdrawal", pointing at a form to print and carry.

**JPN has built an AI assistant with a dedicated death module.** It is live on their website, with quick actions for Daftar Kematian and Semak Status. It answers JPN questions well. It cannot tell KWSP anything.

**MyDigital ID has almost 10 million accounts and will soon be required for death certificates.** The Digital Minister described its scope himself:

> "It is purely a verification and authentication platform. It only verifies your identity by cryptographically linking your digital ID to existing records in a secure one-time process."
>
> Gobind Singh Deo, Minister of Digital, reported by The Star, 13 December 2025

It proves who you are, once, at each agency's door in turn. It carries nothing between them.

**Say it exactly this way:** the capability exists at every one of these agencies. The bereavement path was simply never built.

## Part 4: The Family Is Timed By Rules It Cannot Read

**This is our most original point and no other team will have it.**

| Agency  | Publishes The Deadline?                    | Publishes What Happens If You Miss It?                                                                                                  |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| KWSP    | **Yes.** Six months from the date of death | **Yes.** The RM2,500 Death Assistance is lost                                                                                           |
| JPN     | **Yes.** The seventh day after the death   | **No.** Its Late Registration procedure applies only to deaths before 1 December 2011, and Peninsular Malaysia has no Delayed procedure |
| PERKESO | **No.** Only "tempoh masa yang ditetapkan" | **No**                                                                                                                                  |

**Two of the three agencies enforce timing they do not fully publish.**

A family cannot meet a deadline it cannot find. **That reframes the whole pitch: this is not an inconvenience, it is an unfair process.** Use that word carefully and only once, but use it.

## Part 5: Parliament Said This First

You do not have to assert that bereavement obstructs paperwork. **The state has already said so, on the record.**

The 2016 amendment to the Births and Deaths Registration Act recorded **72,929 late death registrations between 2003 and 2014**, roughly 6,000 a year, and gave its reason for extending the reporting window:

> "Due to the needs, circumstances, rituals, religious obligations and customs with regard to the management of a death that may prevent a qualified person from informing the death to the Registrar..."

**Parliament diagnosed our problem and fixed one seventh of it.** They extended one deadline, at one agency. They never touched the journey.

## Part 6: Scale, And The One Number To Handle Carefully

| Figure                              | Value                            |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Deaths registered in Malaysia, 2024 | **198,992**                      |
| Median age at death, 2024           | **69.0 years**                   |
| EPF death withdrawals, 2024         | **85,532, worth RM3.24 billion** |
| EPF Death Assistance payments, 2024 | **27,006, worth RM67,515,000**   |

**27,006 against 85,532 is 31.6 per cent.** Most EPF death withdrawals are not accompanied by a Death Assistance payment.

### Read This Before You Put That Number On A Slide

**Do not say 68 per cent missed the deadline.** Death Assistance also requires the member to have died **before age 60**, and the median age at death is 69, so the age condition explains a large share of the gap on its own. EPF also calls the payment discretionary.

**The safe claim is the gap plus the silence, never a cause:**

> Most EPF death withdrawals are not accompanied by a Death Assistance payment, and EPF publishes no breakdown of why.

Say that and you are unassailable. Say the other thing and a judge who knows the scheme will take the room off you.

## Part 8: The Four Challenges, And The Four Answers

**Every member should be able to say these from memory.**

**"Isn't this the MyGOV chatbot?"**
A chatbot tells you what to do. This does it. And JPN has already built the chatbot. It still cannot reach KWSP.

**"Isn't this the UK's Tell Us Once?"**
They notify. We claim. Notification stops a family being asked again. It does not put money in their hands and it does not protect a deadline.

**"Doesn't MyDigital ID already do this?"**
The Minister says it is purely verification and authentication. It is our foundation, not our competitor, and it is about to be mandatory for exactly our users.

**"Aren't they all in one building at UTC?"**
Twelve cities, against 199,000 deaths a year. At UTC Perak, JPN and PERKESO are on Level 3 and KWSP is a self-service kiosk on the ground floor. And in the same building you still queue three times and leave with three reference numbers. **A shared roof is not a shared record.**

## Part 9: The Guardrail That Makes It Safe

**Submitted, receipted and tracked. Never approved.**

The agency keeps every eligibility and approval decision. MyTakziah pre-fills forms and cites published rules. It does not decide.

**This is the answer to "what happens when your AI is wrong".** If the system never decides, being wrong costs a correction, not a family's money.

It is also why PERKESO can only ever be shown as **"potentially eligible, confirmation required"**. Their Survivors' Pension turns on a contribution history that neither the family nor we can see: at least 24 monthly contributions within the 40 months before death, or a fractional equivalent. A widow cannot compute that. Neither can we.

## Part 11: Say This, Never That

**Getting this wrong costs more than getting it vague.**

| Never Say                                 | Say Instead                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Approved" or "granted"                   | "Submitted", "received", "being processed"                                            |
| "You are eligible"                        | "Potentially eligible, confirmation required"                                         |
| "You will receive RM2,500"                | "The RM2,500 Death Assistance is at EPF's discretion"                                 |
| "68 per cent miss the deadline"           | "Most death withdrawals carry no Death Assistance payment, and EPF does not say why"  |
| "The same documents three times"          | "The same core information, demanded again each time"                                 |
| "Death withdrawals are rising"            | Nothing. **2022 was higher than both 2023 and 2024.** The series is not a trend       |
| "We integrate with JPN, KWSP and PERKESO" | "The integration is proposed, not proven. We pilot as pre-filled forms"               |
| "MyTakziah is a government service"       | Never imply endorsement. No government logos, crests or wordmarks                     |
| "The seventh day means a fine"            | "The seventh day is JPN's published boundary. The consequence today is not published" |
