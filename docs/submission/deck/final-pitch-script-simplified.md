## Slide 1, Human Hook and Idea

**Presenter A**, 0:00 to 0:52

Good afternoon dear judges and audience. We are Team Survive, a team of students from Universiti Malaya and APU.

To kick off our presentation, we would like to talk about a real case that happened in Malaysia recently.

On the fourteenth of August, Encik Saiful was waiting in Seremban for his wife and their
twelve-year-old son to arrive home.

They were late for a few hours. His calls went unanswered. Then he saw footage online of a tanker crash on the
Temerloh road.

He already knew.

The pain is already torturous enough. And yet a family in his position must then deal with the aftermath processes and claims across three government agencies, on top of the funeral itself.

His grief is the reason we created MyTakziah - Takziah meaning condolences, which our team would like to
express with this project.

In short, MyTakziah is a platform to consolidate the aforementioned three government agency visits and applications into one digital app.

In fact, we already have a working prototype for this app - the brochure reaching you includes its QR. Do scan it during Q&A or judging, and have a look through the website yourselves.

## Slide 2, Problem and Stakes

**Presenter A**, 0:58 to 1:47

Depressingly, En Saiful's case is just one of many. An unspeakable tragedy for him, but a mere statistic on a national scale. Malaysia recorded 49,827 deaths last quarter. On average, that is 548 deaths a day.

For every individual that passed, their close ones have to visit JPN, KWSP and PERKESO separately.

Across these visits, they have to prove their relationship with the deceased three times. The proof of death, three times. Marriage and bank
details, twice more.

And the support is time-limited: they have six months to claim KWSP's RM2,500 Death Assistance, and twelve months to claim PERKESO's funeral benefit of up to RM3,000. If they misfile a claim, or did not report the passing at JPN within seven days - they may even get penalized.

And this is not a problem for some subset of Malaysians. Every one of us will face it, someday. Malaysia was classified an ageing nation in 2021. The Ministry of Finance expects us to be an aged nation by 2048.

## Slide 3, Solution Journey

**Presenter A**, 1:53 to 2:39

MyTakziah isn't built for only tech-savvy people in mind. Half of Malaysians who die are 69 or older, hence our fundamental aim is to make the system as streamlined and intuitive to use as possible.

As shown in these screenshots. We built this for someone who may be in their seventies themselves, doing this for the first
time, alone. MyTakziah turns those tasks into one guided journey.

First, the user starts from an existing record - that has been filed into the system by a hospital, DBKL, or PDRM.

Then, the user provides particulars of the deceased once (who they are, who you are, proof of death, relationship details, bank numbers, etc)

Third, MyTakziah tells them what comes next - every deadline and every form across the three agencies, ordered by whichever expires first.

Fourth, the AI pre-drafts each form from the particulars already given. The family reviews and approves - nothing is sent without their consent, and every decision still belongs to the agencies.

Finally, all three claims sit on one timeline - what's done, what's pending, what's next - on one page, instead of three hotlines.

This is MyTakziah - from three separate visits to three minutes of filing.

## Slide 4, Innovation

**Presenter A**, 2:45 to 3:16

Look at the top half. That's him in the middle, and three agencies around him asking for the same
MyKad, the same death certificate, the same marriage certificate. Three separate pending statuses,
and no way to tell which one is stuck.

Now the bottom. Same documents, handed over once.

The innovation isn't the AI. It's that this is built around one event, a death, instead of around
three agencies.

He still reviews and consents. The agencies still decide.

## Slide 5, Feasibility and Business Model

**Presenter B**, 3:22 to 4:12

We did not start with AI. We started with the forms.

We mapped every repeated field, document and deadline across three agencies, then built the
prototype around that evidence. Version one prepares drafts without a government API, so we can
prove value before integration.

MyTakziah is free for families and funded by partners. Insurers can receive more complete
claims; funeral providers can offer better aftercare.

The RM100 and RM200 figures are price hypotheses to test, not signed contracts.

Our next step is a 100-family pilot in Petaling Jaya through funeral and khairat partners. We
will measure completion, time saved and missing documents. That evidence supports our first
paying partners, then authorised integration.

## Slide 6, Impact and Potential

**Presenter B**, 4:18 to 4:55

We will not call downloads impact. We will call it impact when families complete the right
claims faster, with fewer missing documents and less uncertainty.

Nearly 199,000 families face a death each year, and with an ageing population, more every year.

Once the core journey works, it can extend to Tabung Haji and ASNB, then connect to national
digital rails with the right authorisation.

Singapore already does a version of this with MyLegacy. Malaysia can build a broader one around
our own institutions.

## Slide 7, Team, Ask, and Close

**Presenter B**, 5:01 to 5:43

Two of us are IT students and two are statistics students. This is not our first time shipping:
we have already built a production web application, NexHack, used by close to 1,000 people, as well as
a prototype for a similar website except meant for Malaysian subsidies called Layak, which won us
champion in another hackathon.

Over the next six months, we shall aim to pilot MyTakziah with 100 families.

We are asking this room for the pilot partners, PDPA and legal guidance, and funding to run that
test responsibly.

Grief is already hard enough. Families should be allowed to grieve, not become a broker
between government agencies.

We are Team Survive. MyTakziah: one loss, one guided journey.

Thank you.
